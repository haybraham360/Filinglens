New to this? Here's the 30-second version.

Companies that sell shares to the public have to tell everyone how they're doing, every year, in detail. Those reports are free to read, but they run to a hundred pages or more of careful legal language. That's the problem FilingLens solves.

10-K
A company's official yearly report. Think of it as an annual check-up: what they do, how much they made, and what worries them.
The SEC
The US government agency that makes companies publish these reports, and keeps them online for anyone to read.
Risk factors
The section where a company lists everything that could hurt its business. Often the most revealing part of the report.
Revenue vs. profit
Revenue is all the money coming in from sales. Profit is what's left after paying for everything.
Margin
How many cents of each dollar of sales the company keeps. A 25% margin means 25¢ kept from every $1.

AI that shows its work

Chatbots are confident even when they're wrong. FilingLens is built so you can check every answer in seconds.

  • It shows the receipt

    Every claim links to the sentence in the report it came from. Before you see an answer, FilingLens checks that each quote really appears in the report, word for word.

  • It never makes up numbers

    AI is bad at arithmetic, so it isn't allowed to do any. Every figure comes from the company's own reported data, and percentages are worked out by ordinary code.

  • It says "the report doesn't say"

    Ask what next year's sales will be, and it won't guess. If the answer isn't in the reports, it tells you so.

For the technical crowd

FilingLens is also an engineering and research project. Answers are tested against a hand-checked set of questions on every change, and only improvements ship.

The research side asks an old question with new tools: when a company rewrites its annual report a lot, does its stock do worse afterwards? A 2020 study, Lazy Prices, found that it does. FilingLens repeats that test, and adds AI-based measures of change.

Questions people ask

Do I need to know anything about finance?

No. Ask the way you'd ask a friend. Answers come in plain English, and the numbers page explains each figure in one sentence.

Is this investment advice?

No. FilingLens tells you what companies said in their reports. It doesn't tell you what to buy or sell.

Which companies can I ask about?

Right now: Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Tesla, JPMorgan Chase and Coca-Cola, with yearly reports going back to 2020. More are on the way.

Where does the information come from?

From EDGAR, the US Securities and Exchange Commission's public database. It's the same source professional analysts use, and every answer links back to it.

What if the answer is wrong?

That's why every answer comes with its source. Tap the numbered tag to read the original sentence. If a quote couldn't be found word for word, FilingLens marks it as unchecked.

Does it cost anything?

No. There's no account and nothing to pay.
